# Positioning guide + honesty rules

How to turn a fit analysis into a defensible positioning angle that makes the
candidate look like the right hire — **without overclaiming**. This guide drives
sections 2 and 4 of `analysis-template.md`. The honesty rules below are
non-negotiable and govern the entire analysis.

---

## HONESTY RULES (read first — these override everything)

1. **Every "Meet" must cite profile evidence.** A requirement is "Meet" only if the
   profile contains concrete, named evidence (a role, an achievement, a skill listed
   as *core*). If the evidence is "none", it is not a Meet.
2. **Gaps are labeled "Lack", plainly.** Never hide a gap, soften it into "Partial"
   without basis, or omit a requirement to avoid an awkward row.
3. **Never inflate "Familiar" into "Meet".** A skill in `skills.familiar` supports at
   most "Partial". Core skills support "Meet". Don't promote working knowledge into
   mastery.
4. **Never fabricate a qualification.** Do not invent experience, numbers, tools, or
   outcomes the profile does not support — not in the table, not in the positioning
   angle, not in the talking points.
5. **Talking points frame; they never fabricate.** You may present real experience in
   its most favorable, role-relevant light. You may not manufacture experience to
   fill a gap. If a talking point would require something the profile lacks, mark it
   as a line the candidate must supply (or drop it).

These rules protect the candidate from over-claiming in an interview — and an honest
analysis is a stronger portfolio signal than a flattering one.

## The one idea everything serves

Every strong positioning answer finds **the question the evaluator is secretly
asking** and answers *that*, not the question printed in the posting. The posting
asks for "5+ years of X"; the evaluator is really asking "can this person actually do
*this* job, given who they obviously are?" Locate that hidden question first;
positioning is in service of it.

## Deriving the positioning angle (section 2)

### a. Pick the lead experiences
Match the posting's **top 3 stated priorities** (what it emphasizes, repeats, or lists
first) to the profile's **strongest evidence** (signature achievements, core skills,
most relevant role). Lead with 2–3 experiences that hit those priorities hardest.
Don't lead with what the candidate is proudest of if it isn't what this role needs.

### b. Why now
Infer **why this role exists right now** from the posting and any company context. A
role created mid-downturn → efficiency is the real brief. A role created after a
funding round → scaling is the brief. This usually reveals the strategic spine.

### c. The strategic spine
State the **one sentence** that organizes the whole angle: it connects *what the
candidate would do* to *why this business needs exactly that, right now*. Stress-test
it against the evaluator's commercial reality (budget, deadline, market pressure).

**The reframe move:** if the natural spine plays to the candidate's strengths but
sidesteps the evaluator's real worry, don't accept the either/or — keep their strength
as the *method* and make the evaluator's need the *result*. (E.g. "capability-first"
becomes "capability-building, proven by efficiency this year." Same work; now it
answers the question the panel actually has.)

### d. Handle the gap as a "because", not a "but"
The named weakness, done right, *raises* credibility. Three rules:
1. **Name something they'd spot anyway** — demonstrating accurate self-awareness, not
   confessing a secret.
2. **Attach the counterweight in the same breath** — the asset that makes the gap
   survivable.
3. **Make it a "because", not a "but"** — the gap *explains why* the candidate's
   approach is the right one ("builder-first, which is *why* the plan is built on
   transfer, not heroics").

Discipline: **name the gap once, early, with full force — then be the role, don't
apologize for not being it.** One clean, counterweighted statement; never return to it.

## Tailored talking points (section 4)

- **Resume tweaks:** concrete bullet edits that surface the lead experiences for *this*
  posting's language. Reorder/reword real bullets; don't add fictional ones.
- **Cover-letter hooks:** 1–2 openers tied to this company's "why now", in the
  candidate's real voice — flowing prose, not a framework dump.
- **Screening Q&A:** 2–3 likely questions (derived from the posting's priorities and
  the named gap) with draft answers. Leave 1–2 spots for genuinely candidate-specific
  detail (real numbers/examples) and hand those back to the user to fill — those lines
  prove the answer isn't templated.

## The absolute don'ts
- Fabricating or inflating any qualification (see honesty rules).
- Arriving with a finished prescription instead of diagnosing the role first.
- A generic angle that could be pasted into any company — tie it to *this* situation.
- Over-confessing the gap, or naming several weaknesses.
- Promising miracles on a short horizon.
